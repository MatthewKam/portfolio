## The problem

When a customer went to cancel a subscription, they landed on a page that started a
cancellation-prevention flow. It asked a series of questions, and based on the answers the
merchant could offer a discount, a free gift, a less frequent delivery schedule, or the option
to delay or skip their next order.

The idea was sound, but the product was a mess. Merchants were reporting bugs constantly: what
they built in the landing page builder didn't always match what customers saw on the live
site, dates and numbers sometimes didn't line up, and some flows worked on the legacy customer
portal but broke on the new one.

## What I did

I spent almost six months on bug duty for cancellation prevention. I worked through the
reports merchants sent in, tracked down the cause of each one and fixed it, cleaning up the
experience as I went.

## The hard part

There were three versions of the truth: the builder preview, the legacy customer portal and
the new one. A flow could look right in the builder and be wrong on the live site, or work in
one portal and fail in the other, so I had to hunt down every place they diverged and make
them behave the same.

## Result

Merchant complaints about cancellation prevention dropped by 80%. The ones that remained
weren't about bugs; they were requests for more features.
