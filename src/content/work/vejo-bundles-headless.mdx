## The problem

Vejo wanted one central app where customers could track how many blender pods they used and
manage their subscriptions. The bigger vision was social: a place where customers could
recommend blends to each other and share ways to get more out of their Vejo.

Bundles were part of that. The goal was to give customers the best price while letting them
try different blends, building a monthly box that arrived at their door. Neither Shopify nor
our subscription provider supported bundles at the time, so we decided to build them
ourselves.

## Constraints

The team was one junior full-stack developer and me on the frontend. Sales were declining, and
the CEO and product team wanted bundles live in two weeks as part of a push to win customers
back.

## What I did

We met as a team to flesh out the idea and check technical feasibility. I worked with the CEO,
CTO, product and design to agree on the timeline and what we'd deliver, then mapped out the
work with the junior developer so the schedule was realistic.

On the frontend, I architected bundles so they persisted across every view, even after a page
refresh, and made sure every case was accounted for.

## The hard part

Keeping the cart in sync. A bundle could change from the builder, from any product page or
from the cart, and every view had to show the same, correct state through re-renders and
reloads.

## Result

Sales rose 10%, and long-time customers started buying in bulk. Bundles also gave customers an
easy way to try new flavors, and the flexibility to change their bundle every month to suit
what they wanted.

## Flutter app performance

Alongside bundles, I worked on the Flutter app's performance. The slowdown came from leftovers:
the app was still firing a stack of API calls for older products that no longer worked, and
loading libraries that weren't used anymore, and every load paid for them. I tracked down
those leaks and removed them, and initial render in the mobile app went from 8 seconds to 3,
about 60% faster.
